How it works

  1. A round opens Monday and closes Friday night.
  2. During the round, you may name up to three people by email address. The three are final — no edits, no withdrawals, no viewing your list.
  3. The people you name are never told. Not now, not later, not ever.
  4. Saturday morning, if two people named each other, each receives the other's address by email. One message. That is all.
  5. Everything else is deleted, unread and unmatched. Silence.

If fewer than {{ min_cohort }} people sign up, the round is voided and everything is deleted. Nothing is revealed to anyone.

The trust you are extending

Addresses are stored as keyed hashes and encrypted blobs, so a copy of the database alone reveals nothing. But the operator holds the key material and could, technically, reconstruct who named whom. The operator is one person. The graph is destroyed Saturday morning. The source code is public. That is the trust you are extending. If that is not enough — and that is a legitimate position — do not play.

No profiles. No photos. No chat. No browsing. No scores. If you both said it, you both find out. Otherwise, nothing happened.
